Why this is the wedge

Seek will tell you it is a katydid and then not let you keep it. Arter and ObsIdentify only save species. For insects and fungi — where a photograph often cannot support a species — that means the app discards most of what you actually saw.

So the record stores the returned node, at whatever rank it was. Not a species field with a blank in it.

Counting stays honest. Three numbers, not one: records, of which to species, of which to genus or coarser. Collapsing them into a single score would be the quiet dishonesty this whole app is arguing against — and it is how a life list turns into a leaderboard.

A record can be refined later. “A ground beetle” becomes Carabus granulatus when you get a better photo or someone who knows tells you, and the original determination stays in its history rather than being overwritten.

Open question for you: should refining a genus record to species count as a new species tick on the date of the original sighting, or the date it was refined? Birders care about this more than it sounds.
